The household acceptance factor (HAF) is how willing the people you live with are to put up with your lab. You'll see it called the "WAF", or "wife acceptance factor", in older articles and forums, including some in our [archive](/archive/). We use the broader term because labbers and the people they live with come in all kinds.

Whatever you call it, it's worth taking seriously.

## What people notice

- **Noise:** the most common complaint. See [Noise](/housing/noise/).
- **Heat:** a server can warm up a small room noticeably.
- **Looks and space:** hide cables, and keep blinking lights out of bedrooms.
- **Cost:** a surprise on the power bill is hard to explain. See [what a watt costs](/housing/power/#what-a-watt-costs).
- **Outages:** this is the big one. If an experiment takes down the Wi-Fi during a video call or a favorite show, you'll hear about it.

## How to keep the peace

- **Keep home services separate from experiments.** Your router, Wi-Fi and DNS shouldn't depend on the machine you're testing things on. If they do, leave a simple fallback that anyone can use.
- **Schedule risky changes** for when no one needs the network.
- **Write down the basics.** Leave a note that explains how to get back online if you're away. Someone may need to know this in an emergency.
- **Share the benefits.** Ad blocking, media streaming, photo backups and reliable Wi-Fi all make the lab easier to justify.
- **Plan for your absence.** If you depend on the lab for home automation, security cameras or backups, think about what happens if you're not available to fix it.

It's critical to [understand your requirements](/start-here/requirements/) before you buy, and this means taking other people's needs into account.
